How F1 Started

From post-war grand prix racing to today's global championship.

A Short History

Modern Formula 1 began in 1950 with the first World Championship race at Silverstone. Back then, safety rules were basic and cars were nowhere near as fast or complex as today. Drivers accepted risks that would be unthinkable now.

Key Eras

  • 1950s–60s — The pioneers Names like Fangio and Clark built the legend. Constructors like Ferrari and Lotus showed that engineering mattered as much as driving talent.
  • 1970s–80s — Ground effect & turbo Cars got faster and more dangerous. Safety slowly improved after tragedies that forced the sport to change.
  • 1990s–2000s — Schumacher era Ferrari dominated with Michael Schumacher. TV coverage grew and F1 became a truly international product.
  • 2010s–today — Hybrid power units Teams use hybrid engines, huge budgets, and wind tunnels. Hamilton, Verstappen, and Red Bull defined the latest chapter of the sport.

Why History Matters

When you watch a modern race, you are seeing decades of rule changes stacked on top of each other. Tyre blankets, DRS, budget caps — all of it comes from problems the sport faced in the past. Understanding that makes race weekends easier to follow.
